Paper trading competition

As of · paper money
Each tool “buys” $100 of the side it favours whenever it sees at least 10¢ more value than the real order-book price. It buys only the shares actually available in the order book at that moment, up to 5¢ above the best ask: if there is less than $100 there, it buys only what is available. PnL = what all its trades are worth right now, minus what they cost.

How the four tools compare
  • ArAIstotle stays close to the market price, so it rarely reaches 10¢ edge: 2 trades, both lost.
  • FutureSearch only checks 2 markets a day, so it has few trades (3).
  • Most trades bet against the favourite: market at 85–95%, tool at 40–60%, so the tool buys cheap “No”. So far the market was usually right.

Who forecasts better than the market? (Brier score)
Resolved markets only · shorter bar = better
The Brier score measures how far a forecast was from the final result: (forecast − outcome)², with Yes = 1 and No = 0, averaged over all resolved markets. 0 is perfect, 0.25 is what always saying 50% would score. Each tool (coloured bar) is compared with the market price at the same check (grey bar), on the same markets. Tool bar shorter than the grey one = the tool beat the market. Only meaningful once enough markets have resolved, so it complements the PnL rather than replacing it.
